# Softwarity

Softwarity builds software for the people who build software inside companies.
Not consumer applications, not public APIs: the **internal applications** an IT
department carries - the ones with real users, real data, real access rules,
and a small team behind them.

Those teams all end up writing the same things. A login page. A role model. A
user table. An audit trail somebody asked for after an incident. A gateway
configuration nobody dares touch on a Friday. That work is necessary, it is
never the point, and it is where the weeks go.

::: lead
Our answer is to take that half out of the application and put it where it
belongs: in front of it. That is what [Meerkat](/index) is, and it is the
shape of everything we make - the repetitive part becomes a product, so the
part that is yours stays yours.
:::

## What we make

::: cards
### Products

[Meerkat](/index), the app-gateway: one door in front of an internal
application, which takes charge of authentication, access rules, organisations,
routing, quotas and audit. Its ancestor, **Archway**, did the same job on a
Spring Cloud Gateway stack. It taught us what to do differently - and how much
further the idea could be taken.

### Tools

[plug](https://github.com/softwarity/plug) puts a developer's workstation
inside a cluster, so a service running on a laptop answers under its cluster
name and everything else goes on as before.

### Open source

A shelf of Angular components we needed, published rather than copied from
project to project: navigation rails, row actions, loading indicators,
timezone pickers. See [Open source](/softwarity/open-source).
:::

## What you can count on

Putting a gateway in front of your applications is a long commitment, so here
is what it buys you on our side.

**You can check the product before you trust it.** What is built and what is
not is one table in the repository, read from the code rather than from a plan.
Nothing on this site says "coming soon" about something that does not exist.

**You are not locked in.** The core is source-available and becomes plain
Apache 2.0 two years after each release, so what you deploy today cannot be
taken away from you. Your whole configuration exports as one file you can read
and replay elsewhere. And the gateway sends us nothing, ever - there is no
telemetry in it.

**When something goes wrong, you reach the people who wrote it.** There is no
support tier between you and the code.
